Business question & goals

Using the public Kaggle IBM HR Analytics dataset representing a fictional company (1,470 employee records, 237 departures, 16.12% overall attrition, and an average monthly income of 6,502.93), this project identifies the strongest patterns behind employee turnover.

The analysis segments retention and attrition across overtime workload, department and job role, monthly income differences, job satisfaction, work-life balance, and tenure at the company.

Analysis pipeline

Workflow from raw CSV inspection through SQL querying, Excel Pivot validation, and Power BI DAX modeling.

Step 01

Raw CSV

Inspected 1,470 employee records covering demographics, department, job role, monthly income, overtime status, satisfaction, and tenure.

Step 02

Excel cleaning

Prepared structured Pivot Tables and interactive slicers in Excel to cross-check segment headcounts and attrition breakdowns.

Step 03

SQL queries

Executed 10 analytical SQL queries measuring overtime impact, role attrition, income gaps, satisfaction levels, and tenure cohorts.

Step 04

Power BI dashboard

Built core DAX measures (Total Retained, Total Attrition, Retention Rate, Attrition Rate) using a green retention and red attrition theme.

Key findings

Dataset baselines: 1,470 employees, 16.12% overall attrition rate (237 employees left), and 6,502.93 average monthly income.

Employees working overtime experience roughly triple the attrition rate (31%) compared to those without overtime (10%).

Overtime status produces the sharpest binary split in employee turnover across the dataset.

Sales Representative records the highest attrition rate at 39.76% (nearly double the next highest role), while R&D is the largest department (961 employees) without having the highest attrition.

Job satisfaction shows a clear inverse relationship with attrition, whereas work-life balance exhibits a non-linear pattern with the lowest attrition at a moderate level.

Higher job satisfaction consistently aligns with lower turnover, while both extremes of work-life balance show higher attrition than the middle tier.

Dataset context & analytical notes

Methodological notes for interpreting these HR metrics:
  • The dataset comes from a public Kaggle HR practice dataset representing a fictional organization (1,470 employee rows).
  • In the company tenure analysis, employee headcount is reported alongside attrition rates for each tenure group so small sample sizes are clearly distinguished from larger cohorts.

How to reproduce

  1. Clone or download the GitHub repository containing the dataset and analysis folders.
  2. Run the table creation and analytical scripts inside the SQL/ folder to reproduce the attrition, overtime, income, role, satisfaction, and tenure breakdowns.
  3. Open the Excel/ folder to explore the Pivot Tables, slicers, and spreadsheet dashboard.
  4. Open the Power Bi/ folder to inspect the DAX measures and interactive retention vs. attrition report.
